# Key Concepts: Change Name — 2026-05-31

## Individual Needs Assessment

- **Personalized Programming**: The right program for an individual matters more than the best program in general, as individual needs trump generic training approaches.

## Injury Prevention and Pain Management

- **Pain as Primary Bottleneck**: Pain is always the primary bottleneck; no training stimulus can compensate for an injury that prevents execution.

## Progressive Strength Development

- **Vertical Jump Performance Priority**: Vertical jump height is a primary performance determinant in volleyball, making jump training central to athlete development.
- **Genetics vs. Controllable Improvement**: Genetics influences jumping ability, but improvement is always within your control regardless of starting point.
- **Strength Non-Negotiability**: Getting brutally strong is non-negotiable; being too strong while jumping regularly is essentially impossible.

## Adaptation and Plateau Prevention

- **Body Adaptation Response**: The body adapts only as much as it needs to survive its environment, requiring variety in training to prevent plateaus.
- **Long-term Progress Perspective**: Failure only occurs if you quit; personal records can come years after apparent plateaus.

## Physics of Movement

- **Opposing Forces in Sailing**: Sailing works through opposing forces: wind pressing against the sails versus water pressing against the keel/rudder, which drive the boat forward.
- **Aerodynamic Lift Principle**: The sail acts like an airplane wing generating lift, while the keel and rudder act as underwater wings resisting lateral movement.
- **Downwind Parachute Effect**: When running dead downwind, sails act like parachutes, employing different physics than upwind or beam sailing.
